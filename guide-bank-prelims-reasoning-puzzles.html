Bank prelims reasoning: puzzles and seating under time pressure

Reasoning in bank prelims is a race against a 20-minute sectional timer. Here is the time you actually have, and how to spend it.

Key insights

  • IBPS PO and Clerk prelims: 35 reasoning questions in 20 minutes, about 34 seconds each.
  • SBI PO prelims: 30 reasoning questions in 20 minutes, 40 seconds each.
  • A 5-question puzzle you crack in 4 minutes is cheaper per question than most single questions.
  • Attempt the single questions (inequality, coding, direction) first.

Order of attack

  1. Single questions first: inequalities, coding-decoding, direction sense, blood relations. Each takes 20–40 seconds.
  2. Then the puzzle or seating set that looks most complete.
  3. Leave the set with the most 'not' conditions for last.

Solving a seating set

  • Draw the layout first: a line, circle or rectangle, and note which way people face.
  • Place the fixed facts ("A sits at an end") before the relative ones.
  • When a condition gives two places, draw both cases side by side.
  • Use the negative conditions to eliminate one case.

FAQ

Is there a sectional cut-off in IBPS prelims?

Yes. The IBPS notifications say candidates must qualify in each test. SBI PO Phase I has no sectional cut-off.

How many puzzles should I attempt?

As many as you can finish. A half-solved puzzle usually scores nothing.
