Simple and compound interest: formulas and the CI − SI shortcut

Interest questions appear in almost every Bank and SSC paper. These are the formulas and shortcuts that cover nearly all of them.

Key insights

  • SI grows by the same amount every year; CI grows by the same percentage every year.
  • For 2 years, CI − SI = P(R/100)².
  • For 3 years, CI − SI = P(R/100)²(3 + R/100).
  • Half-yearly compounding: halve the rate and double the number of periods.

The two formulas

Simple interest: SI = P × R × T / 100. Compound interest (annual): A = P(1 + R/100)n and CI = A − P.

Example. ₹8,000 at 5% for 2 years: SI = ₹800; A = 8000 × 1.05² = ₹8,820, so CI = ₹820. The difference is ₹20, which is 8000 × 0.05² = ₹20.

Example. ₹10,000 at 10% for 3 years: SI = ₹3,000, CI = 10000 × (1.1³ − 1) = ₹3,310. The difference is ₹310 = 10000 × 0.01 × 3.1.

Half-yearly and quarterly

For half-yearly compounding, use R/2 per period and 2n periods. ₹10,000 at 10% a year for 1 year, compounded half-yearly, becomes 10000 × 1.05² = ₹11,025, against ₹11,000 with annual compounding. For quarterly, use R/4 and 4n.

FAQ

When is CI equal to SI?

For the first year with annual compounding, they are equal. They differ from the second year on.

What does 'doubles in n years' tell me under SI?

It means R × n = 100, so the rate is 100/n percent.
