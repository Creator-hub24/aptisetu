Negative marking: when does a guess pay off?

A short, exact way to decide whether to guess, using each exam's official marking scheme.

Key insights

  • A guess pays when your chance of being right is above penalty ÷ (marks + penalty).
  • CUET (+5/−1) has a low break-even of 1 in 6, so an educated guess usually pays.
  • Under CAT-style +3/−1, a blind guess on 4 options is worth exactly 0 on average.
  • Bank and SSC sectional cut-offs make a few extra wrong answers more costly than the averages suggest.

The one formula you need

Say a correct answer earns M marks and a wrong one loses N. If your chance of being right is p, the expected score of attempting is pM − (1 − p)N. This is positive when p > N / (M + N). That value is the break-even probability. Above it, attempt. Below it, skip.

Eliminating options changes everything

Each option you can rule out raises p. Under +3/−1 with 4 options, a blind guess is worth 0. Remove one option and it is worth +⅓; remove two and it is worth +1. Under CUET marking, removing two options makes a guess worth 5 × ½ − 1 × ½ = +2.

When the average misleads

Expected value is an average over many questions. In a single paper, wrong guesses can pull a section below its cut-off. IBPS PO and IBPS Clerk prelims require you to qualify in each section. So guess when you have eliminated options, not across a whole section in the last minute.

FAQ

Should I guess blindly in CUET?

The maths says a blind guess on 4 options is worth +0.5 on average under +5/−1, but an educated guess is far better. Use the time to eliminate options first.

What is the penalty in IBPS exams?

One quarter of the marks assigned to the question, as stated in the IBPS notifications.

Are TITA questions penalised in CAT?

Not in recent CATs. The 2026 bulletin does not state the marking.
