CAT 2026: the pattern, and how to plan each 40 minutes

What the official CAT 2026 bulletin actually says about the test, what it leaves out, and a simple way to spend each locked 40-minute section.

Key insights

  • CAT 2026 is on Sunday, 29 November 2026, in three sessions (official bulletin).
  • Each section is locked for exactly 40 minutes. Time you save in one section cannot be used in another.
  • The bulletin does not publish question counts or marking. Check the official mock before test day.
  • In DILR, choosing the right sets matters more than speed.

What the bulletin confirms

The CAT 2026 Information Bulletin and the press release of 26 July 2026 confirm the points below. Anything else you read about CAT 2026 is a forecast.

What it does not say

The bulletin does not give the number of questions or the marking scheme. Recent CAT papers used +3 for a correct answer, −1 for a wrong MCQ and 0 for a wrong TITA (type-in-the-answer) question, but this is not stated for 2026, so treat it as a working assumption. The official mock test on iimcat.ac.in is the best way to confirm the 2026 format before test day.

A plan for each 40 minutes

The lock changes how you should think about time. You cannot bank minutes, so the goal in every section is to use all 40 on the questions you are most likely to get right.

A suggested time plan (our recommendation, not an official rule)
SectionFirst passSecond passLast 3–4 minutes
VARCRead the passages you find comfortable first, about 6–8 minutes each including questionsVerbal ability (TITA para-jumbles, odd sentence out)Answer any TITA you narrowed down; TITA has no penalty
DILRSpend 3–4 minutes scanning every set and rank themSolve the two or three easiest sets fullyPick up single questions from a half-done set
QATake every question you can solve in under 2 minutesReturn to the ones you marked as doableCheck TITA answers for unit and rounding errors

Why blind guessing on MCQs is not worth it

With +3 for a correct answer and −1 for a wrong one on a 4-option MCQ, a blind guess has an expected score of 3 × ¼ − 1 × ¾ = 0. It costs time and adds nothing on average. If you can eliminate one option, the expected score becomes 3 × ⅓ − 1 × ⅔ = +⅓, so an educated guess does pay. TITA questions carry no penalty, so never leave one blank if you have a reasonable answer. See our negative marking guide for the full calculation.

FAQ

How many questions will CAT 2026 have?

The official bulletin does not say. Check the official mock on iimcat.ac.in for the 2026 format.

Can I go back to VARC after it ends?

No. Each section is locked for 40 minutes and you cannot switch while answering a section.

Is there negative marking for TITA questions?

In recent CATs there was none for TITA. The 2026 bulletin does not state the marking, so confirm it with the official mock.
