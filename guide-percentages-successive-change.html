Percentages: successive change, reversals and the fraction table

Most percentage questions in CAT, CUET and Bank/SSC papers use one of four ideas. Learn them once and you can solve these questions fast.

Key insights

  • Two changes of a% and b% give a net change of a + b + ab/100 percent.
  • A 20% fall needs a 25% rise to recover, not 20%.
  • Knowing 1/7 ≈ 14.29% and 1/9 ≈ 11.11% saves a lot of division.
  • A rise followed by an equal fall always leaves a net loss of a²/100 %.

Successive change

If a value changes by a% and then by b%, the overall change is a + b + ab/100 percent. Use a negative number for a decrease.

Example. A price rises 20% and then falls 10%. The net change is 20 − 10 + (20 × −10)/100 = +8%. Check: 100 → 120 → 108.

Example. A rise of 30% followed by a fall of 30% gives 30 − 30 − 900/100 = −9%.

Getting back to the start

After a fall of x%, the rise needed to recover is x / (100 − x) × 100 percent.

Price and consumption

If the price rises by r% and spending must stay the same, consumption must fall by r / (100 + r) × 100 percent. A 25% price rise needs a 20% cut in use.

FAQ

Is a 10% rise then a 10% fall the same as no change?

No. The net is 10 − 10 − 100/100 = −1%, a 1% loss.

Why is the percentage on the old value?

Percentage change is always measured against the starting value, which is why the recovery rise is larger than the fall.
