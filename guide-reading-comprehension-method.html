Reading comprehension: a method that works for CAT and CUET

RC rewards careful reading. Read once, carefully, then answer from the text.

CAT CUET Updated 27 Oct 2026

Key insights

  • Read once, carefully. Rereading the whole passage costs more than reading it well.
  • Write a 3–5 word note for each paragraph's job.
  • Correct options are supported by the passage, not by general knowledge.
  • Extreme words (always, never, only) are usually wrong.

Read for structure

As you read, ask what each paragraph does: does it state a view, give an example, raise an objection or conclude? Write a short note such as "P2: example, the railways". Most main-idea questions are answered by these notes.

CAT and CUET differences

In CAT, RC is part of a 40-minute VARC section and you can choose the order of passages. In CUET (UG), the English paper tests reading comprehension using factual, literary and narrative passages (official bulletin), at a pace of 50 questions in 60 minutes, or 72 seconds a question. That pace means shorter, faster reads.

FAQ

Should I read the questions first?

Glancing at them can help you notice the right lines, but don't skip the reading itself.

How do I improve speed?

Practise reading with notes. Speed comes from understanding structure, not from skimming.
