CAT Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning: Games with rules · B2D-16

TITA · Hard

Data set: The doubling game (Hard)
The doubling game

Four players, A, B, C and D, play four rounds of a game with coins. Each round has exactly one loser, and every player loses exactly one round. The loser of a round pays each of the other three players as many coins as that player holds at that moment, so each of the other three doubles their holding. The loser always has enough coins to pay. No other coins change hands.

  • After the four rounds, A, B, C and D hold 16, 32, 48 and 64 coins respectively.
  • At the start, B held more coins than the other three put together.
  • At the start, C held fewer coins than each of the others.
  • D lost a round before A did.
What is the largest number of coins held by any one player at any time (at the start or at the end of any round)?

Type in the answer (TITA): no options.

Show answer & solution

Correct answer: 104

Solution: The total, 160 coins, never changes. Work backwards from the end: in the round a player loses, every other player's holding is halved (undoing the doubling) and the loser gets back what he paid. Undoing the rounds for each of the 24 possible orders of losers shows that the round-1 loser always starts with 81 to 84 coins and everyone else with at most 44, so 'B held more than the other three together' means B lost round 1. Of the six orders with B first, only (B, D, A, C) and (B, A, D, C) leave C with the fewest coins at the start, and 'D before A' picks the first. Undoing the rounds for B, D, A, C gives starting holdings A 21, B 82, C 13, D 44. Forward check: after round 1 (B loses) 42, 4, 26, 88; after round 2 (D loses) 84, 8, 52, 16; after round 3 (A loses) 8, 16, 104, 32; after round 4 (C loses) 16, 32, 48, 64 ✓. The largest holding is C's 104 coins after round 3.
