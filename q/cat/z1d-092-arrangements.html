CAT Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning: Arrangements · Z1D-092

TITA · Hard

Data set: Lecture week (Hard)
Lecture week

Six lectures by Prof. Sen, Prof. Rao, Prof. Pillai, Prof. Mehta, Prof. Khan and Prof. Bose are held on six consecutive days, Monday to Saturday, one per day. Each lecture is in a different hall, Hall A, Hall B, Hall C, Hall D, Hall E and Hall F, and each covers a different topic, comets, fungi, glaciers, monsoons, volcanoes and tides.

The following is known:

  • Prof. Pillai lectures on monsoons.
  • Either the lecturer on tides is scheduled on Wednesday or the lecture in Hall D is scheduled on Tuesday, but not both.
  • There are exactly 4 days between the days of the lecturer on volcanoes and the lecturer on fungi.
  • Prof. Rao lectures on tides.
  • The lecturer on tides and the lecture in Hall C are scheduled on consecutive days.
  • The lecture in Hall D is scheduled on the day immediately before the lecture in Hall A.
  • Prof. Sen and the lecture in Hall F are scheduled on consecutive days.
  • Either Prof. Sen is scheduled on Monday or the lecturer on tides is scheduled on Saturday, but not both.
  • The lecture in Hall B is scheduled on the day immediately before the lecturer on glaciers.
  • The lecture in Hall E is scheduled on either the first or the last day.
  • Prof. Bose is scheduled on some day before the lecturer on monsoons.
  • There are exactly 3 days between the days of Prof. Mehta and the lecturer on volcanoes.
How many people are between Prof. Khan and Prof. Bose?

Type in the answer (TITA): no options.

Show answer & solution

Correct answer: 1

Solution: Step 1: “Prof. Pillai lectures on monsoons.” Now fixed: Prof. Pillai: monsoons. Step 2: “Prof. Rao lectures on tides.” Now fixed: Prof. Rao: tides. Step 3: “The lecture in Hall D is scheduled on the day immediately before the lecture in Hall A.” Note it; it rules out some placements but fixes nothing on its own yet. Step 4: “The lecture in Hall B is scheduled on the day immediately before the lecturer on glaciers.” Note it; it rules out some placements but fixes nothing on its own yet. Step 5: “There are exactly 4 days between the days of the lecturer on volcanoes and the lecturer on fungi.” Note it; it rules out some placements but fixes nothing on its own yet. Step 6: “There are exactly 3 days between the days of Prof. Mehta and the lecturer on volcanoes.” Note it; it rules out some placements but fixes nothing on its own yet. Step 7: “Either the lecturer on tides is scheduled on Wednesday or the lecture in Hall D is scheduled on Tuesday, but not both.” Note it; it rules out some placements but fixes nothing on its own yet. Step 8: “Either Prof. Sen is scheduled on Monday or the lecturer on tides is scheduled on Saturday, but not both.” Now fixed: Prof. Sen on Monday. Step 9: “The lecture in Hall E is scheduled on either the first or the last day.” Note it; it rules out some placements but fixes nothing on its own yet. Step 10: “The lecturer on tides and the lecture in Hall C are scheduled on consecutive days.” Note it; it rules out some placements but fixes nothing on its own yet. Step 11: “Prof. Sen and the lecture in Hall F are scheduled on consecutive days.” Now fixed: Prof. Rao on Wednesday; Prof. Sen: Hall E; Prof. Rao: Hall B; Prof. Mehta: comets. Step 12: “Prof. Bose is scheduled on some day before the lecturer on monsoons.” Now fixed: Prof. Pillai on Friday; Prof. Mehta on Tuesday; Prof. Khan on Saturday; Prof. Bose on Thursday; Prof. Pillai: Hall D; Prof. Mehta: Hall F; Prof. Khan: Hall A; Prof. Bose: Hall C; Prof. Sen: fungi; Prof. Khan: volcanoes; Prof. Bose: glaciers. Every possible arrangement was checked by computer and exactly one fits all the clues: Monday: Prof. Sen (Hall E, fungi); Tuesday: Prof. Mehta (Hall F, comets); Wednesday: Prof. Rao (Hall B, tides); Thursday: Prof. Bose (Hall C, glaciers); Friday: Prof. Pillai (Hall D, monsoons); Saturday: Prof. Khan (Hall A, volcanoes). Prof. Khan (Prof. Khan) is in Saturday and Prof. Bose in Thursday, so 1 person is between them.
