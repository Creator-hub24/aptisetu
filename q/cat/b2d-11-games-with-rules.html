Data set: The unique-number game (Hard)
The unique-number game

Four players, P, Q, R and S, play three rounds of a game. In each round every player secretly writes a whole number from 1 to 4; different players may write the same number. When the numbers are revealed, a player scores the number he wrote if nobody else wrote that number in that round; otherwise he scores 0 for the round.

  • In every round, at least two players wrote the same number.
  • Q wrote 4 in round 1.
  • In round 2, exactly three players wrote the same number.
  • P scored in round 3.
  • R wrote a different number in each round.
  • At the end of the three rounds, the total scores were P 4, Q 1, R 5 and S 2.
  • The twelve numbers written add up to 33.
Which players scored in round 1?
AR and S only
BP and R only
CR only
DQ, R and S only
Show answer & solution

Correct answer: A. R and S only

Solution: Count scoring turns. R's 5 must come from two different numbers (1 + 4 or 2 + 3), so R scores in exactly two rounds; P, Q and S each score at least once. That is at least 5 scoring turns. A round with a repeated number has at most 2 scorers, and round 2 has exactly 1, so there are at most 2 + 1 + 2 = 5. Hence exactly 5: P scores once (4, in round 3), Q once (1), S once (2), R twice, and rounds 1 and 3 have two scorers each. Q wrote 4 in round 1 but scored only 1 overall, so Q did not score in round 1; P scored only in round 3. So the round-1 scorers are R and S, and Q's 4 was matched by P: round 1 is P 4, Q 4, S 2, R a with a = 1 or 3. If R scored again in round 3 (and Q in round 2), the totals of the numbers come out to 32 + 2b for a whole number b, never 33 — impossible. So Q scored 1 in round 3 and R scored alone in round 2 with 5 − a. If a = 3, R's numbers 3, 2 and his round-3 number (which must pair with S, avoiding 4 and 1) clash; so a = 1 and R wrote 4 in round 2. Round 2: P, Q, S wrote the same d; round 3: P 4, Q 1, R and S both c (c = 2 or 3). Sum: 11 + (3d + 4) + (5 + 2c) = 33 gives 3d + 2c = 13, so c = 2 and d = 3. Final: round 1 — P 4, Q 4, R 1, S 2; round 2 — P 3, Q 3, R 4, S 3; round 3 — P 4, Q 1, R 2, S 2. A computer check of all 4¹² possibilities confirms it is the only one.
