Narrative passage: The last bench

Arjun's family had moved cities in the middle of the school year, and on his first morning he walked into Class IX with his bag held tightly in front of him, as if it were a shield. The teacher pointed to the only empty seat, on the last bench by the window. He sat down and looked at the board without really reading it.

At the break, the others drifted out in noisy groups. Arjun stayed, pretending to search his bag. He knew nobody here, and he had decided, quite firmly, that it was easier not to try.

"You can have the window seat," said a voice. A boy with a patched blue bag had stopped beside the bench. "I always sit here, but nobody comes this far back anyway." He laughed, as though it were a joke against himself. "I'm Imran. We have maths next. Mr Rao gives homework before he even says good morning."

Arjun almost smiled. "Is it hard?"

"Only if you're alone," said Imran, and pulled out a spare notebook. "Copy the last class's notes from mine. I'll show you which ones matter."

By the end of the day Arjun had stopped holding his bag like a shield. He did not know yet whether he would like the city or the school. But the last bench, he thought, was not quite as far from everything as it had looked that morning.

Imran 'laughed, as though it were a joke against himself'. This suggests that he:
Ais friendly and makes light of his own loneliness at the back
Bis making fun of Arjun
Cis proud of the last bench
Ddislikes the other students
Show answer & solution

Correct answer: A. is friendly and makes light of his own loneliness at the back

Solution: He jokes that nobody comes to the back bench, including himself. The joke is on him, not on Arjun, which shows warmth.
