Ratio, mixtures and alligation

Alligation is a quick way to find a weighted average. Once you see that, most mixture questions become one-line calculations.

Key insights

  • Alligation: cheaper : dearer = (d − m) : (m − c).
  • After replacing a fraction f of a mixture n times, the original liquid left is (1 − f)n.
  • In a partnership, profit is shared in the ratio of capital × time.
  • A ratio a : b means the parts are a/(a+b) and b/(a+b) of the whole.

Ratios as parts of a whole

If ₹1,200 is shared in the ratio 3 : 5, the shares are 1200 × 3/8 = ₹450 and 1200 × 5/8 = ₹750. In a partnership, A invests ₹40,000 for 12 months and B ₹60,000 for 6 months. The ratio is 480,000 : 360,000 = 4 : 3.

The alligation rule

Mix a cheaper item at price c with a dearer one at d to get a mixture at m. The quantities are in the ratio (d − m) : (m − c).

Example. Rice at ₹40/kg and ₹55/kg is mixed to sell at ₹45/kg (cost). The ratio is (55 − 45) : (45 − 40) = 2 : 1. Check: (2 × 40 + 1 × 55)/3 = ₹45.

Repeated replacement

A vessel holds 40 litres of milk. 4 litres are taken out and replaced with water, and this is done three times. The fraction removed each time is 4/40 = 1/10. The milk left is 40 × (9/10)³ = 29.16 litres.

FAQ

Does alligation work for percentages and speeds?

Yes, it works for any weighted average: concentrations, average marks, or average speeds over equal times.

Why does the ratio look 'crossed'?

Each quantity is proportional to how far the other price is from the mean, which is why the differences swap sides.
